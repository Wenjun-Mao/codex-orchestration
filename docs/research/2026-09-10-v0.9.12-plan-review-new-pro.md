## Recommendation

**I recommend approving this plan with two small amendments to its acceptance tests. The architectural direction is right; it does not need another redesign or planning cycle.**

The draft addresses the central issue from the earlier review: **settlement is established before choosing an executable source, historical identity is separated from live-checkout requirements, and actual admission—not inspection alone—consumes the shared proof.** It also correctly separates permission to start fresh work from permission to delete history. 

The two amendments I would make are:

1. **Explicitly test that settled history cannot bypass a pending refresh handoff through ordinary activation.**
2. **Exercise candidate-generated history → planned stable admission in the isolated regression before publishing stable**, while retaining both earlier namespaces.

These are small extensions of decisions already in the plan, not additional architecture. Checkpoint 1’s evidence-sufficiency exit should remain a hard prerequisite for implementation. 

## What the plan gets right

### It consolidates interpretation without transferring ownership

“One read-side classification” is appropriately paired with “facts stay with their owners.” That matters: a helper that simply centralizes all the old checks in one large function would relocate duplicated authority logic rather than eliminate it. This plan instead asks execution, assignment/reporting, iteration/reclamation, and preservation owners to supply their respective facts. It explicitly rejects both “closed means fully retired” and “missing means lawfully removed.” 

The scope is also bounded correctly: immediate classification consumers, necessary owner-level readers, one ADR, and an explicit accounting of replaced predicates. It does not authorize a broader lifecycle rewrite. 

### It tests the actual failure topology and goes beyond admission

The proposed journey creates **two normally completed predecessors, reclaims both coordinator roots, preserves the old namespace, then admits and completes useful work under the candidate**. That removes the surviving-selected-checkout escape hatch in the old tests. Assignment readiness, real locator registration, normal closure, and retirement are included, so success cannot stop at an active lifecycle entry while reporting remains unusable. 

Using a frozen v0.9.11 package and allowing fakes only at the external host seam are important protections against a falsely passing fixture. Keeping the new journey coordinator-only is sensible: executor dependency semantics do not need to be replayed to prove this particular boundary. 

### It treats the release ending as a separate gate

The explicit installed RC-to-stable journey is a major improvement. The plan does not confuse successful candidate execution with successful promotion, and it does not allow an immutable stable tag to be rewritten after a failure. The two-task live allowance and escalation rules also constrain recovery loops without redefining partial success as completion.  

## Amendment 1: make pending-handoff precedence explicit at ordinary admission

**This is an implementation ambiguity to close, not a newly reproduced defect.**

Decision 3 says pending handoffs retain their distinct authority and lifetime requirements. However, the explicit negative-case list does not name a pending handoff, and the “direct activation without prior inspection” requirement is primarily a positive journey.  

The distinction to protect is:

> **“All predecessor executions are settled” does not necessarily mean “this repository has no outstanding cutover obligation.”**

A valid historical classification must not become a shortcut around an existing handoff. In particular, a caller must not obtain different permission by skipping `refresh inspect` and invoking ordinary `run activate`.

I would add this to Checkpoint 2:

> **Pending-handoff precedence:** prove that historical settlement does not bypass an existing refresh handoff. Inspection retains the handoff’s existing routing rules, and ordinary activation without the matching refresh authority cannot prepare or admit unrelated target state. Exact consumption/replay remains governed by the existing handoff state and authority checks.

The test should establish that a nonmatching ordinary activation does not create target runtime, workflow, or lifecycle authority and does not clear or replace the handoff. It should also preserve the existing exact consumption/replay behavior; this is **not** a proposal to blanket-reject every operation whenever a handoff file exists.

Use an existing handoff fixture for the relevant state rather than building another complete lifecycle suite. This makes the plan’s existing “distinct authority” decision executable.

## Amendment 2: test candidate-generated history before stable publication

The automated journey currently requires frozen v0.9.11 → candidate, followed by candidate completion and reclamation. The first explicitly required **RC → stable admission** appears in the live ending, after stable promotion/tagging/pushing.  

That leaves a specific coverage gap: an implementation could correctly recognize the retained RC4 and frozen v0.9.11 histories, yet fail to recognize the history produced by its own new RC. The plan explicitly names RC4 and v0.9.11 support, but the classifier also has to support the candidate’s terminal contract under the next package. 

There is a second benefit to extending the automated journey: it exercises **multiple settled predecessor namespaces**, not merely multiple runs in one predecessor namespace.

I would add:

> **Pre-promotion successor check:** after the candidate-created assignment completes and is reclaimed in the isolated journey, inspect and ordinarily admit work under the planned stable package identity while both the frozen-release namespace and candidate-RC namespace remain intact. Run this focused check against the intended promotion contents before stable publication. It does not replace acceptance of the actually installed stable artifact.

The resulting topology is:

```text
frozen v0.9.11 history
    + reclaimed v0.9.12 RC history
        → ordinary v0.9.12 stable admission
```

The live stable ending should likewise preserve and classify both the original RC4 history and the newly completed RC history, consistent with the plan’s instruction to leave predecessor records unchanged. 

This does **not** require another live task or another full-suite run. Extend the same isolated fixture. The final target needs only the small operation necessary to demonstrate usable admission; it does not need another mixed-executor lifecycle.

The frozen-release rule remains intact: do not substitute candidate code for the released predecessor. Building the proposed RC and stable target packages from the proposed delivery contents is a different matter.

## Checkpoint 1 is the real go/no-go boundary

I would not add another checkpoint. I would make sure the existing one produces concrete answers to the following questions, rather than merely reproducing the `ENOENT`.

### What proves standalone status?

The plan correctly states that a missing assignment file cannot prove that no assignment ever existed. That is an important unresolved contract question, not something implementation should resolve with a convenient absence check. 

Checkpoint 1 should identify the supported standalone case and the existing evidence/invariants that distinguish it from incomplete assignment-backed history. It should not “solve” backward compatibility by requiring newly introduced metadata that the retained RC4 records cannot contain.

If existing evidence cannot distinguish a relevant case safely, the plan already gives the correct response: name that exact gap and pause for a design decision. Do not silently classify it as standalone.

### Which identities govern historical execution and retired resources?

The contract needs to distinguish the immutable runtime repository identity from the effective resource identity used for authorized reclamation. The plan already calls for exact execution bindings and effective resource identities; implementation must make that join explicit rather than treating whichever path still exists as authoritative. 

For the retained case, the proof should identify the actual run, assignment execution binding, accepted report, iteration/coordinator member, captured result, and applicable retirement evidence. Missing or contradictory joins should not fall through to a different run or a more permissive reader.

### Which preservation rule applies to each supported contract?

The modern rule is stated well: **the exact captured result must remain preserved in its authorized primary destination; primary may advance, but arbitrary reachability is insufficient.** 

Preserving genuine legacy rules is compatible with one classifier, provided those rules are bounded evidence readers feeding shared facts—not separate caller-specific routing policies.

One documentation caution: if a legacy reader retains a stricter exact-ref rule, do not advertise the modern “primary may advance” behavior as universal. Put that distinction in the single ADR already planned, rather than leaving it implicit in tests.

### Which existing predicates disappear?

Checkpoint 1 already requires identifying what is removed or delegated. Enforce that requirement. The implementation should demonstrate that inspection, sibling checks, preparation, and admission no longer each decide independently what terminal history means. 

The shared component should return facts and evidence references. Each operation can still apply its own permission:

| Operation                    | What historical settlement contributes                                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inspection                   | Whether history requires source-owned action or permits a fresh route, subject to other guards.         |
| Fresh activation             | A revalidated prerequisite for admission—not a replacement for handoff, ownership, or collision checks. |
| Explicit refresh preparation | Recognition that fully settled history has no execution to transfer.                                    |
| Namespace removal            | Relevant historical facts, **not** sufficient deletion authorization.                                   |

That is the boundary the plan intends. A universal `settled ⇒ allow` flag would undermine it.

## Test and release execution notes

**Keep the consolidation requirement as written.** Generate positive historical records through public operations, then reuse fixture machinery for focused negative cases. Do not add another complete canary for every missing digest, wrong binding, or conflicting attachment. Retain distinct legacy, cancellation, crash/replay, and deletion coverage when it is not actually replaced by the new journey. 

**Treat “full suite once” as one required settled run, not an exemption from impact-based reruns.** The draft already permits proportional reruns after subsequent changes. A late change to shared authority interpretation may affect more than the immediately edited test file; a documentation-only change generally will not. That is an evidence-impact decision, not a fixed rerun count. 

**Keep the live allowance as a budget, not a promise of success.** The plan correctly says that a substantive live contract gap pauses promotion and requires escalation. The same principle applies if the second task cannot establish the stable ending: preserve the evidence and report the incomplete gate rather than adding an unauthorized third task or claiming release acceptance.  

## Final assessment

**No fundamental redesign is needed.** This is a bounded delivery plan that addresses the earlier failure mechanism, the duplicated interpretation problem, and the missing public admission endpoint.

My recommendation is to add the **pending-handoff admission test** and the **pre-publication candidate-history-to-stable check**, then proceed through the existing evidence-sufficiency checkpoint. I would not require additional parallel lanes, another framework, or another broad audit.

I have not independently inspected the newly referenced consultation synthesis, current branch divergence, or retained local canary records, and I have not run tests for this review. Those remain evidence to establish during the planned checkpoints—not facts supplied by reviewer agreement.
