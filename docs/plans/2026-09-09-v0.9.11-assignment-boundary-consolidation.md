  ownership/preservation, quiet reporting, and distinct archive/reclamation facts.

### Root cause and evidence disposition

The live RC3 executor's launch recorded its authenticated ready identity and
completed activation, while its iteration member retained a provisional identity.
Closeout correctly refused to archive that unresolved member. The start command
does not publish membership; registration keys membership using a mutable launch
view, and equivalent creation-result replay regenerates equality-bearing times.
An attempted ready-result rewrite also conflicts with original creation provenance.
These are launch-to-iteration contract problems, not a reason to weaken cleanup.

Use the consultation's stable-reference and authoritative-resolution design.
Local source inspection additionally confirms that completed start replay still
passes deadline and original-pristine-baseline checks before its early return.
Therefore start replay alone cannot repair downstream bookkeeping after useful
work. Reproduce the late-reconciliation and interrupted-publication cases locally.
Missing-member effects on cancellation/refresh and unknown-host orderings remain
verification obligations, not assumed newly reproduced defects.

### One live identity authority; durable cleanup progress

- The persisted launch owns immutable launch intent, original creation provenance,
  authenticated executor identity, and completed Git activation. The iteration
  references that launch and owns durable cleanup obligations and progress.
- Logical executor membership uses the owning assignment and canonical launch
  reference, validating namespace/state root, run, contract, and coordinator
  bindings. Reuse the existing immutable launch identity; do not hash a returned
  view or lifecycle observations into a second changing identity.
- Keep authenticated owning-host identity stable. Opaque/unknown creation
  observations cannot downgrade it; contradictory known hosts fail closed.
- Share one resolver between publication and consuming operations. Read the
  persisted authoritative launch under the appropriate existing synchronization,
  not a stale caller-supplied view. Invoke publication after authenticated start
  and creation reconciliation; support recovery when either side persists first.
- Status must describe effective identity and absent/stale projection without
  silently becoming a mutation endpoint. Authorized closeout/reconciliation uses
  the same resolution and may persist converged membership before deciding
  eligibility. Cover a missing first member, not only a stale existing member.
- Discover obligations through exact assigned launches/execution bindings, not
  only materialized iteration rows. Cancellation, refresh, and cleanup cannot
  infer no executor merely because publication was interrupted.
- Preserve registered time, ready identity, ownership, archive attempts, and
  cleanup progress monotonically. Never resurrect a cancelled assignment,
  append unrelated work, choose a winner among duplicates, downgrade ready state,
  or overwrite newer progress with a stale view.
- Ordinary archival still requires the applicable start/activation, result,
  verification, preservation, and fresh host/Git checks. Keep the existing bounded
  stalled-provisional recovery path separate. After lawful source retirement,
  completed cleanup must retain its necessary evidence without an indefinite
  dependency on a deleted live launch file.

This replaces full-view membership hashing and competing cached identity
decisions. Do not turn the entire iteration into a transient view: durable
archive/reclamation progress still belongs in its existing records. No new
registry, ready-confirmation command, or generic synchronization engine.

### Replay and compatibility boundaries

Same-creation-result replay compares semantic classification, known host,
ready/provisional ID or opaque digest/length, and applicable selector values.
Preserve the first recorded event timestamps and populated observations across
equivalent replay. Reject conflicting populated facts; omission cannot erase
known evidence. Apply this to both selector and creation evidence. A later ready
result is not an equivalent replay of an original provisional response. Do not
rewrite that provenance or relax freshness checks for deletion observations.

Separate already-authenticated identity resolution from first-time activation.
After a commit or expired launch window, resolving identity must not rerun branch
attachment, reset work, renew a claim, or broaden fresh-start protections. If a
supported start replay completes bookkeeping, authenticate the original actor
and operation and preserve their original evidence. Consumers must still work
from durable completed authority without requiring that replay.

Define an explicit reader policy for the known prior member format/producers
before changing identity validation. Prefer preserving existing IDs, original
digest provenance, registered time, and archive progress while uniquely resolving
the stable reference. Do not compare an old view digest with today's launch view
or pretend a digest reconstructs its original bytes. Supported reconciliation
requires exact assignment/run/namespace/launch ownership and necessary creation,
start, and Git evidence; missing provenance or duplicate/conflicting owners block.
Use existing locks and preserve lock order; host actions stay outside long-held
locks. Document the supported boundary and compatibility exit condition, rather
than rekeying historical records or adding broad migration/RC rescue machinery.
