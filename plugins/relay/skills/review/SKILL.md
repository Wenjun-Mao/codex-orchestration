# Review the Outcome and Direction

Turn evidence into a better decision. Did the work achieve the agreed
result? Does the current direction still serve it? What should happen
next?

A project wrapper adds domain evidence and priorities. Direct or the
wrapper decides when review is due; this skill explains how to review.
It adds no timer or extra approver.

## Choose the Depth the Decision Needs

- **Checkpoint:** assess a consequential decision during active work.
  Decide whether to continue, correct, change direction within existing
  permissions, or close the affected work.
- **Final assessment:** state whether the agreed outcome was achieved
  and which evidence decides it. A straightforward success can be brief.
- **Retrospective:** when requested, or when consequential failures,
  costly success or unresolved causes warrant it, reconstruct enough
  decisions and costs to explain what happened and improve the next
  attempt. A status summary is not a full retrospective.

Do not repeat reviews that already answer the decision. Reuse valid
checks and inspect further where uncertainty or contradiction matters.
This review is not a fresh round of independent product acceptance.
A separate reviewer is optional unless the user, plan or applicable
requirements call for one.

## Recover What Success Was Supposed to Mean

Read the original approved outcome and acceptance criteria, explicit
later amendments and the latest user direction. Identify the intended
result, what evidence would be enough to prove it, and what assistance
is allowed when that affects the claim.

Recover current state from existing records. Older failures or holds
do not override newer authorized decisions. Read the evidence and
decisions relevant to this review, rather than whole transcripts by
default.

Judge the actual agreement first:

- **The plan was insufficient.** Every required deliverable could pass
  while the promised result remained false. Explain the gap in the
  acceptance checks; do not blame compliant execution for an unstated
  requirement.
- **Execution is incomplete.** Required evidence is missing, or the
  assistance or repairs used mean the evidence no longer proves the
  claimed result. Identify what is still needed.
- **The agreed outcome was achieved.** Recognize bounded success,
  including planned human judgment or a supported negative answer to
  an agreed investigation. Do not add a larger ambition afterward.

For example, suppose the agreed goal is unattended operation. A plan
that checks only individual components may never prove that goal, even
if every check passes. If the plan required an unattended trial but
nobody ran it, the missing proof is an execution gap. If the agreement
instead retained a human release decision, that planned involvement
is not itself a failure.

Planning and execution defects can coexist. Useful partial results
remain useful, but publication, cleanup or a narrower artifact cannot
make an unmet outcome complete. Discuss broader limitations when they
change the interpretation, safety or next decision. Do not obscure a
clear verdict with unrelated caveats.

## Explain the Causes That Matter

For consequential gaps or costly successes, establish what happened,
where the problem belongs, and which evidence supports the explanation.
It might belong in the product, its documentation, the workflow, or the
way an existing instruction was applied. Compare credible competing
explanations; keep suspected causes hypothetical until evidence
separates them.

Distinguish missing guidance, failure to apply existing guidance, a new
defect and a capability that was unavailable. When a problem recurs,
trace the earlier recommendation into the next plan and actual action.
Was it applied, contradicted by new evidence, or merely acknowledged?
Check which guidance was available at the time. A repeated miss calls
for understanding that decision before adding another instruction.

After repairs, identify what evidence the agreed claim still needs.
Require fresh independent use only when independence is part of that
claim. Direct arranges any already-authorized follow-up checks.

When assessing effort, count consequential coordination, reconstruction
and rescue. Planned review is not automatically rescue. Separate
observed costs from estimates and waiting. Missing measurements limit
claims about savings; they do not invalidate unrelated correctness.

## Consider Whether the Next Repair Is the Right Move

Look across accumulated failures, changing dependencies and local
successes that may hide drift from the overall outcome. Treat the next
repair as one candidate, rather than the automatic next step.

Compare credible alternatives when they could change the decision:
fixing the component responsible, preventing the problem upstream,
reusing existing work, changing the sequence, trying a representative
complete workflow, or stopping or deferring. Do not invent options to
meet a count or manufacture new work.

Consider the remaining work through integration, proof, review and
closeout, including uncertainty and ongoing upkeep. A bounded
investigation can be worthwhile before its eventual benefit is proved.
An estimate is not a deadline or quota; preserve real user limits and
native controls. Retain usable gains, supported limits and negative
findings. Continuing unchanged is a valid recommendation when the
evidence supports it.

## Return a Judgment Someone Can Act On

Lead with whether the agreed outcome was achieved and the decisive
evidence. Explain material gaps or causes, useful gains, and your
recommendation. Say what should happen next, what evidence would change
your recommendation, and which dependent work can proceed or still
needs to wait. Include consequential unknowns without treating every
unknown as a blocker. Match the depth to the request and decision.

Keep the result in the existing plan, review section or conversation.
Link evidence instead of copying inventories. No fixed report format
or new ledger is required.

When a lesson changes the method, identify the existing instructions,
code or process that should change, and the action it should improve.
Editing a skill or passing a desk test does not by itself demonstrate
better results in actual use.

Review does not launch work, change scope or control a Goal. Return to
Direct for already-authorized continuation; an internal review adds
no routine approval gate. Ask for a user decision only when the next
action requires one. Preserve active ownership, safe cleanup, actual
deadlines and explicit pauses while assessing the work.
